1. ICOG Panel Rigging, Crane Transport & Platform Erection

In utility-scale solar generation plants, Inverter Control and Outgoing (ICOG) cubicles serve as the vital interface connecting modular DC strings to central power transformation and substation collection networks. These heavy, sensitive electrical assemblies require meticulous rigging, stable transport logistics, and unyielding foundation support.

Today's site operations documented two critical operational stages of ICOG deployment: road transit and elevated platform erection. First, an Escorts F15 mobile hydraulic crane was deployed to transport the heavy, weather-wrapped cubicle across the graded site access road at Sidegallu, maintaining slow, stabilized speeds to protect sensitive internal relays and switchgear components. Second, our rigging crew hoisted and lowered the unit directly onto an elevated structural steel platform anchored atop robust reinforced concrete plinths at Hulikunta.

Rigorous Crane Rigging & Placement Protocol:

  • • Mobile Transit Logistics: Crane transport maintained along compacted site roads with low boom angles to minimize load pendulum oscillations.
  • • Certified Web Slings: High-capacity synthetic web slings secured to factory lifting eyelets to prevent structural distortion of the cubicle shell.
  • • Elevated Plinth Defense: Mounting atop raised steel I-beam frames to keep switchgear elevated well above monsoon water pooling, damp soil, and ground dust.
  • • Weather-Wrap Protection: Heavy-duty factory polythene wrapping retained throughout lifting to shield breakers from ambient dust and heat.

2. Module Mounting Structure (MMS) Fixing & Alignment

The structural durability of a photovoltaic solar field rests entirely upon its Module Mounting Structure (MMS). In the rocky red soil terrain of Sidegallu and Hulikunta, AARAA's mechanical crews are actively executing the assembly, fixing, and plumb alignment of heavy-gauge, hot-dip galvanized steel tables.

As visible in the site photographs, our construction personnel are positioning column posts and diagonal sway bracing directly over cast concrete pile foundations. These assemblies are engineered to resist high cyclic wind uplift forces and thermal expansion over decades of field service.

3. Geotechnical Earthworks: Bulk Foundation Excavation & Rocky Cable Trenching

Subsurface civil and electrical Balance of Plant (BoP) works represent two of the most demanding phases of solar farm construction. The geological profile across Sidegallu, Hulikunta, and Chikkajogihalli consists of hard cemented gravel, shallow granite bedrocks, and rocky strata requiring mechanized earthmoving capabilities.

Today's site progress highlighted two key geotechnical activities. First, at the Sidegallu solar block, deep bulk excavation and pit leveling was completed for a heavy electrical inverter/control foundation pad, with site surveyors establishing elevation datum levels and pre-laying subsurface conduits directly on the leveled rock base. Second, along the Iyanahalli – Kannakatti Road corridor, a JCB backhoe excavator fitted with a heavy hydraulic rock breaker was deployed to fracture hard rock strata for continuous, uniform AC power cable trenches.

Deep bulk excavation, survey benchmark check, and conduit bedding preparation for electrical foundation plinth at Sidegallu / Hulikunta.
Mechanical rock breaker fracturing hard strata to achieve engineering trench depths for underground AC power cables at Chikkajogihalli.

Key Subgrade & Trenching Engineering Standards:

  • • Level Subgrade Bedding: Comprehensive subgrade leveling and rock excavation to prevent uneven foundation settlement beneath heavy electrical blocks.
  • • Vibration Control: Controlled hydraulic breaking to protect adjacent MMS pile caps from ground shock or structural micro-fissures.
  • • Conduit Invert Alignment: Continuous gradient maintenance to prevent internal water pooling during the monsoon season.

4. Structural Welding, Joint Alignment & Anti-Corrosive Painting

The elevated steel superstructures supporting Inverter Control Rooms and ICOG equipment demand strict structural engineering controls. Steel members must withstand heavy static equipment loads alongside dynamic lateral wind forces that sweep across Karnataka's open plateaus.

At the Kudligi project front, AARAA's certified structural welders were documented executing full-penetration joint welding and weld-seam grinding across longitudinal and transverse I-beam girders. Following weld quality inspections and de-slagging, our finishing team immediately applied high-build red oxide anti-corrosive primer across all exposed steel surfaces and welded joints.

Certified site welder carrying out precision joint welding on heavy I-beam support girders at Hulikunta, Kudligi.
Site technician applying anti-corrosive red oxide primer coating across the fabricated ICOG platform framework.

This two-tier protective system guarantees that non-galvanized heavy structural steel sections remain impervious to atmospheric rust, monsoonal humidity, and chemical soil action throughout the project's multi-decade lifecycle.

7. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is an ICOG panel and why is it installed on an elevated steel platform?

An ICOG (Inverter, Control, and Outgoing) panel houses primary electrical control circuits, string combiners, and medium-voltage switchgear. Mounting these heavy cubicles on elevated structural steel frames anchored to reinforced concrete pedestals safeguards sensitive electrical hardware from ground-level moisture, monsoon flooding, soil dampness, and blowing sand.

2. How does AARAA Infrastructure handle rocky soil during solar cable trench excavation?

In boulder-strewn and hard rock formations common across Karnataka's renewable regions, AARAA deploys hydraulic rock breakers mounted on JCB backhoe excavators. This controlled mechanical fracturing achieves precise trench depths without disturbing the adjacent MMS pile foundations or causing excessive ground shock.

3. What quality checks are performed during Module Mounting Structure (MMS) installation?

Our quality assurance team verifies coordinate and elevation benchmarks using digital total stations, tests vertical plumbness with spirit levels, applies calibrated torque wrenches to high-tensile fasteners, and checks diagonal sway bracing alignment before approving the tables for module mounting.

4. Why is protective red oxide primer applied to solar structural steel frames?

Solar infrastructure is engineered for a 25-year operational lifecycle under intense ultraviolet rays, high seasonal rainfall, and ambient dust. Applying industrial-grade red oxide primer following joint welding ensures comprehensive corrosion resistance and preserves structural steel strength.
