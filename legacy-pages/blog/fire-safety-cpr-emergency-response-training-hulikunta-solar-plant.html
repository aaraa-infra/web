Strengthening Workplace Safety Through Practical Training

Workplace safety is an essential part of successful infrastructure and renewable energy project execution. Recognising the vital importance of emergency preparedness, AARAA Infrastructure, in association with Continuum Green Energy Limited, successfully conducted a comprehensive safety awareness and practical training session at the Hulikunta ITC-02 Solar Plant on 18 November 2026.

The training programme brought together site workers, technicians, and supervisory personnel to strengthen their understanding of fire prevention, emergency response procedures, and basic life-saving techniques. In high-capacity solar installations with extensive electrical infrastructure and civil works, fast and knowledgeable worker intervention during the first few critical minutes of an incident can prevent injuries and save lives.

1. Fire Extinguisher Training and Practical Demonstration

Fire emergencies can occur unexpectedly at construction and industrial project sites due to electrical faults, hot works, or environmental conditions. Knowing how to respond decisively during the initial incipient stage of a fire helps protect personnel, prevent equipment damage, and safeguard solar plant assets.

The PASS Fire Extinguisher Technique

Participants received structured hands-on training on operating portable fire extinguishers using the internationally recognized PASS method:

P

Pull

Pull the safety pin to break the tamper seal and unlock the operating mechanism of the fire extinguisher.

A

Aim

Aim the extinguisher nozzle or hose directly towards the base of the fire, where the fuel source is located.

S

Squeeze

Squeeze the top handle or trigger lever steadily to release and discharge the extinguishing agent.

S

Sweep

Sweep the discharge nozzle smoothly from side to side across the base of the flames until fully extinguished.

The live demonstration helped workers understand the correct grip, discharge duration, and the critical importance of maintaining a safe upwind distance while tackling fire outbreaks.

2. Heart Attack Awareness and CPR Training

Medical emergencies require instantaneous recognition and an informed response. In high-temperature outdoor solar construction environments, cardiac distress and heat-related collapses can occur. The second phase of the programme focused on heart attack awareness, early symptom identification, and essential life-saving first-aid procedures.

Early Warning Sign Recognition

Workers were taught to identify key symptoms of cardiac distress, including acute chest pressure, radiating discomfort in the left arm or jaw, shortness of breath, sudden cold sweats, dizziness, and unexplained weakness.

Emergency Escalation & Rapid Medical Dispatch

Emphasizing the vital importance of contacting emergency medical services and alerting site safety personnel immediately without attempting unproven folk remedies or delaying transport.

Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR) Protocol

Site personnel were introduced to the fundamentals of basic CPR, understanding how rhythmic external chest compressions sustain vital blood flow to the brain and heart when normal breathing has ceased.

Hands-On Casualty Assessment Demonstration

A practical demonstration showed workers how to evaluate casualty responsiveness, check for normal respiration (ear-to-chest assessment), position hands over the center of the chest, and maintain the correct compression depth and cadence.

This practical training demonstrated that immediate bystander CPR before professional medical transit arrives dramatically improves survival outcomes during critical cardiac emergencies.

3. Active Participation and Practical Learning

A defining highlight of the programme was the enthusiastic and disciplined participation of site workers and supervisory staff in the live demonstrations.

Rather than limiting the session to theoretical classroom instruction, the Safety Department created an interactive learning environment where workers observed simulated casualty scenarios, asked practical questions, and reviewed real-time corrective guidance from HSE officers.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the PASS technique taught during the solar site fire extinguisher training?

The PASS technique is an internationally recognized 4-step method for operating fire extinguishers: P – Pull the pin, A – Aim at the base of the fire, S – Squeeze the handle, and S – Sweep side to side across the base of the fire.

2. Why is CPR and heart attack awareness essential for construction and solar plant workers?

Medical emergencies such as cardiac arrest require immediate intervention before professional medical help arrives. Early symptom recognition and prompt CPR compressions maintain blood circulation to vital organs, significantly improving survival outcomes.

3. Where was this safety training programme conducted and with whom?

The training was conducted at the Hulikunta ITC-02 Solar Plant in Karnataka on 18 November 2026, organized by the Safety Department of AARAA Infrastructure in close association with Continuum Green Energy Limited.

4. How does AARAA Infrastructure promote a proactive safety culture across its project sites?

AARAA Infrastructure prioritizes worker wellbeing through daily safety briefings, practical mock drills, continuous hazard identification, full personal protective equipment (PPE) compliance, and hands-on first aid training across all turnkey civil and solar EPC projects.
