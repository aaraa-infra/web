Building Emergency Response Awareness at the Project Site

Workplace safety is a fundamental pillar of responsible project execution. At AARAA Infrastructure, we continuously emphasize safety awareness, proactive risk mitigation, emergency preparedness, and the physical well-being of our workforce across all construction sites.

On 17 November 2026, AARAA Infrastructure successfully conducted a specialized Snake Bite Emergency Response Mock Drill at the Hulikunta ITC-2 Solar Plant site in Karnataka. The exercise was designed to strengthen workforce alertness, equip ground teams with practical first-aid knowledge, and establish clear evacuation workflows in the event of wildlife-related hazards.

Utility-scale solar EPC construction sites inherently span vast expanses of open terrain, trenching zones, rocky soil, dense ground cover, and outdoor storage yards. Operating in these environments demands strict hazard vigilance, particularly regarding wildlife encounters. The mock drill ensured workers understand the critical sequence: immediate identification, rapid communication with the safety cell, stabilization, and coordinated hospital dispatch.

Key Activities Covered During the Mock Drill

The safety awareness and training session focused on five critical operational areas:

1. Rapid Emergency Identification & Reporting

Workers were trained to instantly identify the signs of a snake encounter or bite incident and immediately notify the designated site supervisor, first-aid officer, and HSE team without panic.

2. Immediate First-Aid Precautions

The session demonstrated practical casualty stabilization: keeping the patient calm, resting the affected limb below heart level, restricting unnecessary body movement, and avoiding dangerous unscientific traditional practices.

3. Emergency Communication and Escalation Hierarchy

A simulated emergency radio/telephonic communication drill was executed, verifying the exact escalation pathway from field spotter to site safety officer Md Kaif and project management.

4. Safe Evacuation & Hospital Dispatch Protocols

A live emergency evacuation simulation was demonstrated, including casualty lifting techniques, placement into the designated on-site medical transport vehicle (Mahindra Bolero Neo), and coordination with the nearest district hospital equipped with antivenom.

5. Prevention & Site Housekeeping Vigilance

Workers received guidance on preventive behavior: wearing mandatory safety gumboots, using flashlights during dusk/dawn transit, exercising caution around module packing crates, clearing vegetative overgrowth, and maintaining tidy material storage areas.

Essential First-Aid Precautions: Do's and Don'ts

Snakebite emergencies are time-critical medical events. Following clinical protocols aligned with the World Health Organization (WHO) and India's National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), site teams were instructed on essential do's and don'ts:

DO's — Crucial Immediate Actions

  • Keep the affected person calm; reassure them that most snakebites are manageable.
  • Immobilize the bitten limb using a loose splint or cloth sling to slow venom spread.
  • Remove rings, watches, tight clothing, or footwear near the affected area before swelling sets in.
  • Note the time of the bite and key visible symptoms.
  • Dispatch immediately to the nearest tertiary medical centre in the on-site emergency vehicle.

DON'Ts — Dangerous Practices to Avoid

  • Never cut, incision, or bleed the wound area.
  • Never attempt to suck venom orally or with mechanical suction devices.
  • Never apply a tight arterial tourniquet (this causes tissue necrosis).
  • Never apply ice packs, potassium permanganate, or herbal pastes.
  • Never waste critical time attempting to capture, pursue, or kill the snake.
Important Medical Note: Polyvalent Antivenom (ASV) and advanced patient stabilization must strictly be administered by licensed medical practitioners at a hospital. Our site focus is 100% on prompt stabilization and immediate transfer.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Why are snakebite emergency mock drills important at solar plant sites?

Solar plant sites often involve open terrain, trenching, and vegetation. Mock drills help workers understand real wildlife risks, overcome panic, practice emergency communication, and execute rapid hospital transport procedures.

2. What should workers do immediately after a suspected snakebite?

Keep the person calm, restrict unnecessary body movement, immobilize the affected limb using a loose splint, and arrange immediate evacuation to the nearest medical facility equipped with antivenom.

3. What should workers avoid after a snakebite?

Workers should strictly avoid cutting the wound, sucking venom, applying tight tourniquets, or relying on unscientific traditional remedies. Immediate hospital transport is the only proven medical solution.

4. Does AARAA Infrastructure conduct regular workplace safety awareness activities?

Yes. AARAA Infrastructure promotes workplace safety across all civil and renewable energy project sites through regular toolbox meetings, safety drills, emergency preparedness demonstrations, and mandatory PPE compliance.
